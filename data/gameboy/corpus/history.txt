The Game Boy is an 8-bit handheld game console made by Nintendo. It was released in Japan on April 21, 1989, in North America on July 31, 1989 and in Europe on September 28, 1990. The Game Boy was designed by Gunpei Yokoi and the Nintendo Research and Development 1 team, the same team that had created the Game & Watch handhelds.

The Game Boy launched in Japan at a price of 12,500 yen. In the United States it cost 89.99 dollars and came bundled with Tetris, a link cable and headphones. Nintendo sold a million Game Boys in the United States within a few weeks of launch.

The model number of the original Game Boy is DMG-01. DMG stands for Dot Matrix Game, because the console used a dot matrix liquid crystal display instead of the fixed segment displays of the Game & Watch.

Gunpei Yokoi followed a design philosophy he called lateral thinking with withered technology. Instead of using the newest and most expensive parts, he used cheap, mature, well understood technology in new ways. The Game Boy had a monochrome screen and a slow processor, but it was cheap, robust and ran for many hours on four AA batteries.

The main rivals of the Game Boy were the Atari Lynx, released in 1989, the Sega Game Gear, released in Japan in October 1990, and the NEC TurboExpress. All three had color screens and backlights, but they were expensive and drained their batteries in a few hours. The Game Boy won because of its long battery life, its low price and its huge library of games, starting with Tetris.

Tetris was the pack-in game of the Game Boy in North America and Europe. Tetris was created by Alexey Pajitnov in 1984 in Moscow. Henk Rogers negotiated the handheld rights for Nintendo. The Game Boy version of Tetris sold about 35 million copies and is the best selling Game Boy game. Its Type A music is an arrangement of the Russian folk song Korobeiniki.

In Japan the Game Boy launched with Super Mario Land, Alleyway, Baseball and Yakuman. Super Mario Land was produced by Gunpei Yokoi and developed by Nintendo R&D1.

The Game Boy and the Game Boy Color together sold 118.69 million units worldwide, making the Game Boy line one of the best selling game consoles of all time. More than a thousand games were released for the system.

In 1995 Nintendo released the Play It Loud series, Game Boys in red, yellow, green, blue, black, white and transparent cases. In July 1996 the Game Boy Pocket followed, a smaller and lighter console with a clearer black and white screen that ran on two AAA batteries. The Game Boy Light, released in Japan only on April 14, 1998, added an electroluminescent backlight and used two AA batteries.

The Game Boy Color was released on October 21, 1998 in Japan and in November 1998 in North America and Europe. It had a color screen, a processor that could run twice as fast, more memory and an infrared port, and it played all the original Game Boy games. The Game Boy Advance, a 32-bit successor, was released in March 2001, and the original Game Boy line was discontinued in 2003.

A Game Boy that was burned in a barracks bombing during the Gulf War in 1991 still works and plays Tetris. It was displayed for years at the Nintendo World store in New York as a symbol of the console's toughness. In 1993 the Russian cosmonaut Aleksandr Serebrov took a Game Boy to the Mir space station and played Tetris in orbit.

Gunpei Yokoi was born on September 10, 1941. He joined Nintendo in 1965 as a maintenance engineer and went on to create the Ultra Hand toy, the Game & Watch series, the cross shaped directional pad, the Game Boy, the Virtual Boy and games such as Metroid and Kid Icarus. He left Nintendo in August 1996 and founded Koto Laboratory, where he designed the WonderSwan handheld for Bandai. Yokoi died in a car accident on October 4, 1997.
