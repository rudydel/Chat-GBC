The Game Boy Pocket was released on July 21, 1996 in Japan. It is smaller and lighter than the original, uses two AAA batteries and has a true black and white screen with less blur. The Game Boy Pocket kept the Game Boy selling until the Game Boy Color arrived.

The Game Boy Light was released on April 14, 1998 in Japan only. It is a Game Boy Pocket with an electroluminescent backlight, powered by two AA batteries.

The Game Boy Color was released on October 21, 1998 in Japan, on November 18, 1998 in North America and on November 23, 1998 in Europe. Its 160 by 144 pixel color screen can display 56 colors at once from a palette of 32,768 colors. The screen is a reflective thin film transistor display without a backlight. Clear black cartridges only work on the Game Boy Color, while clear cartridges run on both the Game Boy Color and the original Game Boy.

The Game Boy Advance was released on March 21, 2001 in Japan, on June 11, 2001 in North America and on June 22, 2001 in Europe. It uses a 32-bit ARM7TDMI processor at 16.78 megahertz and plays Game Boy and Game Boy Color games. The Game Boy Advance sold 81.51 million units. The Game Boy Advance SP of 2003 folds shut and has a lit screen, and the Game Boy Micro of 2005 is the smallest Game Boy.

The Super Game Boy, released on June 14, 1994, is a cartridge for the Super Nintendo that plays Game Boy games on a television with color palettes and borders.

The Game Boy Camera was released on February 21, 1998 in Japan. It plugs into the cartridge slot and takes 128 by 112 pixel photos in four shades of gray. In 1999 the Guinness Book of Records listed it as the smallest digital camera in the world. The Game Boy Printer, also from 1998, is a small thermal printer that prints pictures from the Game Boy Camera on sticker paper.

Pokemon Red and Green were released on February 27, 1996 in Japan. They were developed by Game Freak and created by Satoshi Tajiri. Pokemon Red and Blue reached North America on September 28, 1998 and sold more than 31 million copies worldwide. Pokemon Yellow followed on September 12, 1998 in Japan. Pokemon Gold and Silver, released on November 21, 1999 in Japan for the Game Boy Color, use a real time clock inside the cartridge.

Super Mario Land was a launch title of the Game Boy on April 21, 1989. Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins, released on October 21, 1992 in Japan, introduced Wario. Wario Land: Super Mario Land 3 followed in 1994.

The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening was released on June 6, 1993 in Japan. Link's Awakening DX, a color version with an extra dungeon, came out for the Game Boy Color in 1998.

Kirby's Dream Land, released on April 27, 1992, was the first Kirby game. It was developed by HAL Laboratory and created by Masahiro Sakurai. Kirby is pink, but the American box art showed him in white because the Game Boy screen had no color.

Metroid II: Return of Samus was released in November 1991 in North America. Donkey Kong, released in 1994, is a puzzle platformer with 101 levels and special Super Game Boy enhancements. Kirby Tilt 'n' Tumble, released in 2000 for the Game Boy Color, is controlled by tilting the console thanks to an accelerometer in the cartridge.

Nintendo was founded in Kyoto on September 23, 1889 by Fusajiro Yamauchi as a maker of playing cards. Hiroshi Yamauchi was president of Nintendo when the Game Boy launched in 1989. Satoru Okada of Nintendo R&D1 led the hardware design of the Game Boy under Gunpei Yokoi.

The Virtual Boy, released in 1995, was a tabletop console with red stereoscopic 3D graphics designed by Gunpei Yokoi. It sold poorly and was discontinued within a year. Yokoi's last handheld, the WonderSwan, was released by Bandai in 1999 after his death.
